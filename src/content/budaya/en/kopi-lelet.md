In Lasem, coffee is more than a morning drink. It comes with a small habit found in few other places, and that habit is called **ngelelet**. The story is simple but distinctive, and it makes sense only in a town whose whole life turns around batik.

## Kopi lelet

**Kopi Lelet** is a thick coffee served with sweetened condensed milk. "Lelet" describes its dense, slow-pouring texture, unlike ordinary brewed coffee. It is served in Lasem's traditional warungs, accompanied by morning talk and, often, a cigarette.

## Ngelelet

When the coffee is gone, grounds mixed with condensed-milk residue are left at the bottom of the glass. This mixture is called *lethekan*. From it comes **ngelelet**: the grounds and milk are picked up with the tip of a matchstick and used to draw batik motifs on cigarette paper. A dragon, a phoenix, or a *kawung* pattern may appear there, sketched loosely, with no special tool.

> Ngelelet is not a performance. It happens mid-conversation, every morning, among people who have no sense that they are preserving anything.

## A living heritage

What makes ngelelet interesting is how unofficial it is. It is not taught in a studio or written into a manual. It lives because it is passed on through habit, from one warung table to the next. That is why ngelelet is the most honest expression of Lasem's batik identity: not something put on display, but something that seeps all the way into a cup of coffee.

## Brass craft

Batik is not the only skilled heritage in Lasem. The town is also known for **kerajinan kuningan**, brass worked into household tools, ornaments, and everyday objects. Together with batik and kopi lelet, this craft completes the picture of Lasem as a town that works by hand, not only by machine.

Karang Jahe is a white-sand beach in Punjulharjo village, about 7.5 kilometres from the centre of Rembang city. Its name comes from the small rocks along the shoreline whose shape resembles ginger (*jahe*) root. Opened to visitors in 2014, it has become one of the busiest coastal destinations in the regency, especially at weekends and on public holidays.

## White sand and casuarina

What sets Karang Jahe apart from many beaches on Java's north coast is its colour. The shoreline runs for roughly three kilometres, with bright sand and water that stays calm. Rows of casuarina trees (*cemara*) were first planted along the edge to hold back erosion, and today they give visitors natural shade. Under the trees, families spread mats to sit and eat together.

## What you can do

Besides swimming in the shallow water, visitors can hire a motorboat, try an ATV or mini trail ride, or use the children's play area. Food stalls, a prayer room, gazebos, and photo spots line the site. Part of the shore is used for mangrove planting, so a visit here also offers a look at efforts to protect the coastline.

## Getting there

From central Rembang, head east towards Lasem. The entrance sits on the main Semarang–Surabaya road at kilometre 7.5 of the Rembang–Lasem stretch, at the Punjulharjo village gate. From the gate the beach is about 800 metres away. It is open daily, morning to late afternoon.

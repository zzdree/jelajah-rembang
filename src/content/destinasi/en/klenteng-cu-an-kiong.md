Cu An Kiong Temple stands at Jalan Dasun number 19, in the Soditan Chinese quarter of Lasem. Its name means "Palace of Peace and Compassion". Local tradition holds that it is among the oldest temples on Java, built in the 16th century with teak that was then plentiful around Lasem.

## Architecture and Mazu

The building follows the southern Chinese temple style, with a timber frame. Its two main teak columns are believed never to have been replaced. The main altar to **Tian Shang Sheng Mu**, another name for Mazu, the sea goddess, sits in the rear hall and is not open to the public, following the hierarchy of sacred space in Chinese tradition.

On the temple's door leaves are painted two Chinese-Lasem figures, Bi Nang Un and his wife Na Li Ni, remembered for teaching batik techniques to local residents. The detail reflects how closely the Chinese and Javanese communities of Lasem were bound.

## A living heritage

The temple is still active and cared for by the local Chinese community. Its atmosphere differs from a historic building kept only as a monument: incense is still burned and worship still takes place. The surrounding Soditan quarter is itself a heritage kampong of old Chinese-style houses.

## Getting there

From the centre of Lasem town, the temple sits in the Soditan area, reachable on foot or by a short drive. It is well known locally and easy to ask directions to.

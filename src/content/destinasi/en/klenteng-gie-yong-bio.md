Gie Yong Bio Temple stands at Jalan Babagan number 7 in Lasem, on the west bank of the Lasem river. Together with Poo An Bio and Cu An Kiong, it forms a cluster of Chinese places of worship that marks Lasem as Java's "Little China".

## What makes Gie Yong Bio distinct

The building was founded in 1780 and renovated in 1915. Its distinction lies in whom it honours. The temple was built to remember three Lasem heroes: **Tan Kee Wie**, **Oey Ing Kiat**, and **Raden Panji Margono**. Because it venerates a native Javanese figure, Gie Yong Bio is considered unusual, one of the few temples in Indonesia with a *kongco* of indigenous origin.

The reverence the Chinese community of Lasem holds for Raden Panji Margono is evidence of friendship between the two communities' forebears, a trace of the long history of coexistence in this town.

## Around the temple

Set in Lasem's Chinese quarter, the temple can be visited alongside the two others and the surrounding heritage kampong. Old houses, Lasem batik, and the former river-trade district give the building its context.

## Getting there

From the centre of Lasem town, the temple is on Jalan Babagan, reachable on foot or by a short drive. Crossing the Lasem river to the west brings visitors into this area.

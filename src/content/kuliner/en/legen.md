Legen is a drink that comes straight from the tree. It is **fresh sap** tapped from the flower stalk of the **siwalan**, a sugar palm that is a signature tree of Rembang's dry limestone belt.

## The siwalan palm and its belt

The siwalan, or lontar palm, grows well in **Sale and Sluke districts**, an area known as Rembang's siwalan belt. The tree is rarely found outside the region because it suits dry, rocky ground. From its flower stalk, tappers collect the sap that drips through the night.

The sap is then gathered in **bamboo tubes** and sold at roadside stalls. It tastes sweet and slightly cloudy, and has already begun a mild fermentation, which gives it a faint, refreshing sourness. Tapping is usually done at night, so the legen sold in the morning has just come from the tree.

## Best in the morning

Legen is at its best **in the morning**, when it has just been tapped. The longer it is left, the further fermentation goes and the more the taste changes. For that reason it is drunk as a fresh morning beverage rather than kept for long.

> Legen teaches one thing about drinking from nature: its freshness is measured in hours, not days.

## A drink of the dry belt

For people in Sale and Sluke, legen is not only a drink but part of daily life tied to the siwalan palm. Besides the sap, the tree is used for sugar and other products, which makes it one of the traditional supports of the local economy. Legen is often a relaxed morning companion, drunk at a stall over conversation. For a traveller passing through, stopping at a roadside stall for a glass of morning legen is a simple way to meet Rembang.

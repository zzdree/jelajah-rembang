Kelo mrico is a coastal dish whose name already tells the story. In local speech *kelo* means a soupy dish, while *mrico* means pepper. So the moment Rembang people hear the name, they know what is coming: **a yellow fish soup with pepper at the front**.

## Built on dukang fish

The fish used is **ikan dukang**, also known as **ikan sembilang**, a striped eel catfish. Its flesh is soft with a distinct aroma. For some, the fish can taste muddy or fishy if mishandled, and that is where the pepper comes in.

The spice paste includes shallot, garlic, kaffir lime leaf, coriander, turmeric, and candied nut (*kemiri*). Together they produce a fragrant yellow broth, while a generous amount of pepper gives a sharp heat. The pepper is what masks any fishiness and leaves the clean, fresh taste of the fish behind.

## A heat that warms

Kelo mrico is eaten hot, usually straight with **white rice**. The broth should not be left to go cold, because the aroma of pepper comes through best while it is still steaming. For those who like spice, the broth feels light and refreshing rather than heavy.

It is generally served in a bowl, with the fish cut into generous pieces so the flesh does not fall apart. Some stalls add sliced tomato or basil leaves alongside, though the base seasoning stays pepper and turmeric. The white rice balances the heat and soaks up the broth.

> Here pepper is not just a seasoning. It is part of how the coast handles sea fish so the flavour stays clean on the tongue.

## A coastal kitchen heritage

Rembang has a long coastline, and its home kitchens have long been used to working with the sea's catch. Kelo mrico is one expression of that habit: fresh fish, simple spices, and a willingness to use plenty of pepper. The dish is rarely found far from the coast, and is easiest to find at stalls near the harbour and the fish market, where dukang fish is still sold fresh each day.
